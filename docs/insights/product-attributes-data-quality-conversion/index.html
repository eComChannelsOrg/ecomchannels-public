Product Attributes and Data Quality: How They Drive Conversion

Complete, accurate product attributes decide whether listings show in filtered searches and whether shoppers buy. What the data says and how to fix it.5 min read6 cited sourcesUpdated 30 October 2026

Key figure54% of shoppers dropped a purchase due to conflicting product informationSource: salsify.com

Product attributes are the structured facts about an item, such as size, material, colour, weight, compatibility, pack quantity and barcode. They drive conversion in two ways: marketplaces use them to decide whether your listing appears when shoppers search and filter, and shoppers use them to decide whether to buy. Incomplete or inconsistent attributes cost you both visibility and sales, while clean, consistent data across channels does the opposite.

Attributes are how marketplaces find your products

Most marketplace shoppers narrow results with filters: size, brand, colour, price, material, room, compatible model. Those filters only work on structured attributes. If your listing leaves "material" blank, it disappears the moment a shopper ticks "stainless steel".

eBay puts it directly: item specifics play an important role in increasing the visibility of your listings on both eBay and external search engines, and the more data you provide, the better eBay can match your item through search, left-hand filters and category pages (eBay Seller Center). Amazon, Kogan, Bunnings Marketplace, Woolworths Everyday Market and other marketplaces each have category templates with required and recommended fields that serve the same purpose.

The next wave of search raises the bar further. Australia Post's 2026 report advises retailers to optimise for AI discovery with structured, machine-readable content that lets AI agents compare products on fit, price and delivery, and sums it up as "clean your data or get ignored by agentic AI" (Australia Post, 2026).

Bad data loses sales

Shoppers notice gaps and contradictions:

  • Salsify's 2025 research found 54% of shoppers dropped purchases due to conflicting information (Salsify, 2025).
  • Syndigo's 2025 survey of more than 8,500 consumers found 44% had abandoned a purchase due to insufficient product information (Syndigo, 2025).
  • Akeneo's 2025 survey found 77% would leave a site to check other channels if information was missing, and 38% will abandon a site if they cannot easily find key features such as sizing and materials (Akeneo, 2025).

On a marketplace, "leaving to check elsewhere" often means clicking a competitor's listing on the same results page.

Where data quality breaks down

For most sellers, the problem is not a lack of information. It is information scattered across supplier spreadsheets, an ERP, a Shopify store and several seller portals, each edited separately. Typical failures:

  • Inconsistency. The website says 120 cm, Amazon says 1.2 m, eBay says 118 cm.
  • Unit and format drift. "Blk", "black" and "Black/Charcoal" in the same catalogue.
  • Missing required fields. Listings rejected, suppressed or excluded from filters.
  • Wrong identifiers. Incorrect barcodes cause catalogue mismatches or listings attached to the wrong product.
  • Inconsistent specs across similar products. Baymard's 2026 research found 30% of sites have severe issues with specs across products of the same type, and 64% have poor or mediocre spec sheet performance (Baymard Institute, 2026).

Building a reliable attribute set

  1. Create one master product record. Every fact lives in one place, with one approved value.
  2. Standardise units and vocabularies. Choose metric units, a fixed colour list and consistent naming.
  3. Map to each channel's template. The same master value may need to go into differently named fields on each marketplace.
  4. Prioritise filterable attributes. Fill the fields marketplaces use for filters and that shoppers ask about most: size, dimensions, material, compatibility and what is included.
  5. Validate before publishing. Check required fields, allowed values and barcodes.
  6. Close the loop. Customer questions, reviews and return reasons point to missing or unclear attributes. See returns caused by poor product information.

Where AI fits

AI is good at the slow parts of this work: extracting attributes from supplier PDFs and product images, normalising inconsistent values, suggesting missing fields and mapping data to each marketplace's template. It is not a source of truth. Facts such as dimensions, safety ratings and compatibility must come from the product or the supplier, and a person should approve anything the AI inferred. FAQ

Which attributes matter most?

Start with the fields each marketplace marks as required, then the filterable fields shoppers use in your category, usually size or dimensions, material, colour, compatibility and quantity. Customer questions and return reasons show you what is still missing.

Do I need a separate PIM system?

Not necessarily. What you need is a single master record that feeds every channel. Some sellers use a dedicated PIM; others keep the master record in their listing and order management platform.

Can AI fill in missing attributes automatically?

AI can suggest values from descriptions, supplier documents and images, but suggestions should be reviewed. Never publish safety, compliance or compatibility details that have not been confirmed.
