# AI Dynamic Pricing for Online Retailers: Gains, Risks and Rules

> How AI dynamic pricing works for online retailers, the margin gains reported, Australian Consumer Law and marketplace pricing rules, and how to start safely.

AI dynamic pricing adjusts prices automatically based on demand, stock, competitor prices and costs, and when done well it can lift both sales and margin. McKinsey reports retail clients typically see sales growth of 2-5% and margin increases of 5-10% from well-adopted dynamic pricing. In Australia it is legal, but prices must be clear and not misleading, marketplaces have their own fair pricing rules, and personalised pricing using shoppers' data carries growing reputational and regulatory risk.

## Dynamic, algorithmic and personalised pricing

These terms are often used interchangeably, but they are different:

- **Dynamic pricing** changes prices for everyone in response to market conditions such as demand, supply, time or competitor moves. UNSW researchers describe it as reacting to the market, [with prices rising for everyone when demand peaks](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2025/10/AI-using-data-personalised-data-prices-online) and falling when it eases.
- **Personalised pricing** uses personal data such as browsing history, device or postcode to predict an individual's willingness to pay, so two people may see different prices for the same product at the same time.
- **Repricing** is the marketplace version: software adjusting your offer price against competing sellers, usually within a floor and ceiling you set.

Most small and mid-sized online retailers use, or should consider, the first and third. The second raises fairness and privacy questions that most sellers are better off avoiding.

## What the gains look like

McKinsey's retail pricing work found the impact depends heavily on whether pricing teams trust and use the tool. Where category and pricing managers helped design the system and could override recommendations, retailers saw [sales growth of 2 to 5% and margin increases of 5 to 10%](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/retail/our-insights/how-retailers-can-drive-profitable-growth-through-dynamic-pricing). In one pilot, an Asian online retailer saw a 10% rise in gross margin and a 3% improvement in GMV in the pilot categories.

The same research explains why pricing is not "set and forget". Key-value items, the products shoppers remember prices for, can account for up to 80% of a typical retailer's revenue but only half its profit. Good pricing keeps those items sharp and recovers margin on the long tail, where shoppers are less price-sensitive.

The trust point matters. McKinsey notes that pricing staff often reject black-box recommendations that look far above or below current prices. Tools that explain their logic and let people override them get used; tools that do not are switched off.

## The Australian rules

**Australian Consumer Law.** The ACCC states that [surge or dynamic pricing is not illegal](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/pricing/setting-prices), but businesses must be clear about the price consumers will pay and must not make false or misleading claims about prices. An algorithm that inflates a "was" price before a sale, or shows one price and charges another, creates the same legal risk as a person doing it.

**Drip pricing and unfair practices.** In November 2025 the Federal Government announced reforms to [require mandatory transaction fees to be shown prominently and upfront](https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/andrew-leigh-2025/media-releases/stopping-unfair-trading-subscription-traps-and-hidden), alongside a general prohibition on practices that manipulate consumer decision-making and cause harm. Pricing algorithms should show the full price early, including unavoidable fees.

**Privacy.** Personalised pricing relies on personal information, which brings Privacy Act obligations. Our article on [AI risks and governance for Australian retailers](https://ecomchannels.com/insights/ai-risks-governance-australian-retailers/) covers the automated decision-making transparency rules that start in December 2026.

**Marketplace policies.** Marketplaces also police pricing. Amazon's Fair Pricing Policy, for example, says Amazon [monitors prices and can remove the Featured Offer, remove the offer or suspend selling privileges](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G5TUVJKZHUVMN77V) for practices that harm customer trust, including misleading reference prices, prices significantly higher than recent prices on or off Amazon, and excessive shipping fees. A runaway repricer can cost you the Buy Box or worse. Check each marketplace's own policy before automating.

## How to start safely

1. **Know your true margin per channel.** Marketplace commissions, fulfilment and payment fees differ. A price that works on your Shopify store may lose money on another channel.
2. **Set floors and ceilings** for every SKU based on landed cost plus channel fees.
3. **Start with long-tail or slow-moving lines**, not your most visible products.
4. **Keep humans in charge** of key-value items and promotions, with alerts for large changes.
5. **Keep "was" prices honest.** Only use reference prices that genuinely applied.
6. **Review results weekly:** units, margin, Buy Box share and customer complaints.
7. **Avoid personalised pricing** unless you have legal advice and a clear disclosure approach.

## FAQ

### Is dynamic pricing legal in Australia?

Yes. The ACCC says surge or dynamic pricing is not illegal, but prices must be clear and businesses must not make false or misleading price claims.

### What is the difference between dynamic and personalised pricing?

Dynamic pricing changes prices for everyone based on market conditions. Personalised pricing uses an individual's data to set a price for that person, which raises more privacy and fairness concerns.

### Can repricing tools hurt my marketplace account?

Yes, if they set prices that a marketplace considers unfair, such as prices far above recent levels or misleading reference prices. Set sensible floors and ceilings and review each marketplace's pricing policy.
