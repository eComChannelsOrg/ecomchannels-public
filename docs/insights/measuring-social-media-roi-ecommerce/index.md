# How to Measure Social Media ROI for E-commerce

URL: https://ecomchannels.com/insights/measuring-social-media-roi-ecommerce/
Category: Social commerce
Updated: 2026-10-30

> A practical framework for measuring social media ROI in e-commerce: what to track, why platform attribution overstates results and how to test incrementality.

Social media ROI for an online retailer is the extra profit social activity generates, divided by what it costs, including ad spend, content production and staff time. Measuring it well means tracking sales and margin rather than likes, comparing platform-reported results with your own order data, and testing what is truly incremental. Most marketers are not there yet: Nielsen found [85% feel confident measuring ROI, but only 32% actually measure it holistically across channels](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2025/nielsen-unveils-makerting-roi-blueprint/).

## Why social ROI is hard to measure

Social rarely works as a single click from post to purchase. IAB Australia found online shoppers use [an average of 4.8 discovery touchpoints](https://www.iabaustralia.com.au/resource/ecommerce-report-2025/), from search and retailer sites to friends, other consumers and social media. A shopper might see a TikTok, research on Instagram, read marketplace reviews and then buy on Amazon a week later. No single dashboard sees all of that.

Two problems follow:

- **Over-crediting.** Each platform's attribution report counts conversions that happen after someone sees or clicks an ad, whether or not the ad made a difference. As PPC Land explains, [conversion lift testing exists because attribution reports credit ads with conversions that follow them, whether or not the ads made any difference](https://ppc.land/conversion-lift/). Add up the Meta, TikTok and Google reports and the total can exceed your actual sales.
- **Under-crediting.** Organic posts, creator content and marketplace sales influenced by social often get no credit at all, because the shopper did not click a tracked link.

Organisational issues add to this. In Nielsen's 2025 survey, the top ROI measurement challenge was [stakeholder alignment on key metrics (22%), followed by unclear KPIs and too much data (19%) and incomparable data (19%)](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2025/why-strategy-matters-more-than-tools-roi/).

## What to track

Start with a short list of metrics tied to money, grouped by stage.

**Reach and attention** (is anyone seeing it?)
- reach and impressions per network
- video views and average watch time
- follower growth

**Engagement and intent** (is it interesting?)
- saves, shares and comments, which signal stronger intent than likes
- link clicks and click-through rate
- profile visits and product tag taps

**Commercial outcomes** (is it paying?)
- sessions and revenue from social, tracked with consistent UTM tags
- sales from creator and campaign discount codes
- cost per acquisition and return on ad spend
- new versus returning customers from social
- contribution margin after product cost, fees, shipping and returns

Nielsen found [38% of marketers now prioritise sales or ROI as their top success metrics](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2025/nielsen-unveils-makerting-roi-blueprint/). For retailers, margin is the number that matters: a campaign that sells heavily discounted bulky items with expensive freight can show strong revenue and still lose money.

## Three levels of measurement

**1. Tracked attribution (every retailer).** Use UTM parameters on every link, platform pixels and server-side conversion tracking, and unique discount codes for creators and campaigns. Compare platform-reported sales with orders in your own system each week. The gap tells you how much the platforms over-claim.

**2. Incrementality tests (when spend is meaningful).** A conversion lift test compares a randomly selected group shown your ads with a holdout group that is not. [The difference between the two groups is the lift](https://ppc.land/conversion-lift/), which is the sales your ads actually caused. Meta, Google and TikTok offer lift studies; geo tests, where you pause ads in some regions, are another option.

**3. Marketing mix modelling (larger budgets).** MMM uses aggregated sales and spend data over time to estimate each channel's contribution, including offline and brand effects. Google made its open-source MMM, Meridian, [available to all marketers in January 2025](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/meridian-marketing-mix-model-open-to-everyone/), noting that MMMs measure performance in a privacy-centric way because they rely on aggregated data. MMM typically needs a long run of reasonably clean data and some analytical skill, so it suits growing brands more than new ones.

## Building a simple monthly social ROI report

1. **Pull costs.** Ad spend by platform, creator fees, content and tool costs, and an estimate of staff time.
2. **Pull outcomes from your own data.** Orders and revenue by source, code and channel, including marketplaces where possible.
3. **Calculate margin, not just revenue.** Subtract product cost, marketplace and payment fees, shipping and returns.
4. **Compare with platform claims.** Note the difference and adjust your view of each platform.
5. **Record tests and changes.** Note new creative, offers and lift test results so trends are explainable.
6. **Decide.** Move budget and effort toward what produces margin, and cut what does not.

Data quality is the main obstacle. Salesforce found that [while 83% of marketers recognise the shift toward personalised, two-way engagement, only one in four are satisfied with how they use data](https://www.salesforce.com/marketing/resources/state-of-marketing-report/?bc=OTH). Getting orders, fees and costs into one place does more for ROI measurement than any new dashboard. ## FAQ

### What is a good ROI for social media marketing?

There is no universal figure. It depends on your margins, average order value and how much repeat purchasing social customers bring. Aim for a positive contribution margin after all costs, and compare channels using the same method.

### Are platform-reported conversions accurate?

They are useful for comparing campaigns within one platform, but they usually overstate total impact because each platform counts conversions that would have happened anyway. Compare them with your own order data and use lift tests to check what is incremental.

### How do I measure organic social?

Use UTM-tagged links in bios and posts, unique discount codes, and "how did you hear about us" questions at checkout. Watch for changes in direct and branded search traffic after strong organic periods, which often reflect social influence.
