# Sustainable E-commerce Packaging and AI: A Guide for Retailers

URL: https://ecomchannels.com/insights/sustainable-ecommerce-packaging-ai/
Category: AI in retail
Updated: 2026-10-30

> Where Australian e-commerce packaging stands against national targets, how to avoid greenwashing, and how AI and automation cut waste, freight and returns.

Australian packaging is improving but still well short of national targets: in 2023-24, 86% of packaging was reusable, recyclable or compostable against a 100% target, and only 20% of plastic packaging was recycled or composted against a 70% target. For online retailers, the most effective sustainability steps also save money: right-sized cartons, fewer split shipments, accurate product information that reduces returns, and better stock planning. AI and automation help with each, but any green claim you make must be specific and backed by evidence.

## Where Australian packaging stands

The Australian Packaging Covenant Organisation (APCO) tracks progress against the National Packaging Targets. Its 2023-24 data, released in December 2025, shows mixed results ([APCO, 2025](https://apco.org.au/news/20YOl00000WKxMkMAL)):

- 86% of packaging was reusable, recyclable or compostable (target 100%).
- 20% of plastic packaging was recycled or composted, up from 19% (target 70%).
- Average recycled content was 44% (target 50%).
- Problematic and unnecessary single-use plastics were down 46% from the 2017-18 baseline.

There is progress. Packaging use per person fell from 264kg to 251kg, and paper and paperboard, which makes up more than half of all packaging placed on the market, had a recovery rate of 66%. But APCO says that [on the current trajectory Australia will continue to miss the targets](https://apco.org.au/news/20YOl00000aALxrMAG) without reform, and it is advocating for nationally consistent regulation and Extended Producer Responsibility settings. Brand owners are being asked to invest in credible, auditable packaging data.

For e-commerce sellers, cardboard cartons and plastic satchels are the bulk of what you ship. The targets and likely regulation point the same way: less packaging, more recyclable material, more recycled content and better records.

## What shoppers will actually pay for

Shoppers say they care, but price usually wins. Australia Post's 2025 report found [only 7% of shoppers prioritised sustainability over saving money](https://auspost.com.au/content/dam/ecommerce-report/australia-post-ecommerce-report-2025.pdf) in 2024, and more than two-thirds thought sustainable new goods were too expensive. Instead, many turned to second-hand: the 2026 report says [46% of Australians buy second-hand items each year](https://auspost.com.au/content/dam/auspost_corp/media/documents/ecommerce-reports/annual-ecommerce-reports/ecommerce-report-2026.pdf), with recommerce growing on affordability and sustainability.

The practical lesson: sustainability efforts that add cost for the shopper are a hard sell, while those that remove waste and cost from your operation are easy to justify.

## Avoid greenwashing

Green claims attract regulator attention. When the ACCC swept 247 business websites, [57% made concerning environmental claims](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-greenwashing-internet-sweep-unearths-widespread-concerning-claims), and packaging was one of the sectors where it had active investigations. The ACCC's guidance says [claims must be accurate, truthful and backed by clear evidence](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/environmental-and-sustainability-claims), and that even factually correct claims can mislead through the overall impression, including visuals.

For online sellers this applies to product listings, packaging inserts and social posts. "Eco-friendly packaging" is vague; "mailer made from 80% recycled cardboard, kerbside recyclable" is specific and checkable. AI tools that write listings or posts need the same discipline: a person should check every environmental claim against evidence before it is published. ## Where AI and automation reduce waste

Most packaging and freight waste in e-commerce comes from decisions made order by order. Automation makes better decisions consistently:

- **Right-sized packaging.** When product dimensions and weights are stored accurately, systems can suggest the smallest suitable carton or satchel, cutting material, void fill and cubic freight charges.
- **Fewer split shipments.** Consolidating orders from every channel in one place makes it easier to pick a multi-item order into one parcel rather than several.
- **Smarter carrier choice.** Choosing the right service for the parcel's size and destination avoids over-servicing, such as express freight that was not needed.
- **Fewer failed deliveries.** Accurate addresses and tracking reduce re-delivery attempts and lost parcels.
- **Fewer returns.** Every return doubles the transport and often the packaging. Clear product information, sizing and images help shoppers buy the right item the first time.
- **Less dead stock.** Better demand forecasting reduces excess inventory that ends up discounted or written off; see [AI demand forecasting for retailers](https://ecomchannels.com/insights/ai-demand-forecasting-retail-inventory/).

None of these require a sustainability budget. They lower costs, and the environmental benefit follows.

## A simple plan

1. **Measure:** packaging types, quantities and spend per month; average parcel volume versus product volume.
2. **Record accurate product dimensions and weights** for every SKU.
3. **Rationalise to fewer, better-fitting packaging sizes** with recycled content and kerbside-recyclable materials.
4. **Automate carrier and service selection** by parcel size and destination.
5. **Make claims specific and evidenced**, and review them before publishing.
6. **Track returns by reason** and fix the listings driving them.

## FAQ

### What are the National Packaging Targets?

They are Australia's benchmarks for packaging: 100% reusable, recyclable or compostable packaging, 70% of plastic packaging recycled or composted, 50% average recycled content, and phasing out problematic single-use plastics. APCO reports progress annually.

### Can I call my packaging "eco-friendly"?

Avoid vague terms. The ACCC expects specific, accurate claims backed by evidence. Say exactly what the packaging is made of and how it can be disposed of.

### Do Australian shoppers pay more for sustainable delivery?

Few do. Australia Post found only 7% of shoppers put sustainability ahead of saving money in 2024, so focus on changes that cut waste and cost together.
