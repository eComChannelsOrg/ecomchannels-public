Cut Product Photography Overhead with AI, Without Cutting Corners

Where AI genuinely saves time and money in product imagery, where a real photo shoot still matters, and how to build a lean image workflow for many channels.5 min read8 cited sourcesUpdated 30 October 2026

Key figureNearly 75% of struggling advertisers cite creative as their top hurdleSource: aboutamazon.com

AI cuts product photography overhead most in the work that happens after the shoot: background removal, resizing and cropping for each channel, colour and lighting correction, lifestyle scenes and seasonal refreshes. It does not replace capturing accurate photos of the real product, which you still need for compliance and trust. The biggest saving for multichannel sellers comes from shooting once and letting software produce a compliant image set for every marketplace, with a person approving the result.

Where the photography cost really goes

For a retailer selling on several marketplaces, the shoot itself is only part of the cost. The rest is repetitive handling:

  • Retouching and cut-outs to meet white-background rules for main images.
  • Resizing and re-cropping for each channel's size, aspect ratio and framing rules: square for Amazon and Bunnings, portrait for some fashion channels, extra negative space for Etsy.
  • Variant coverage so every colour and finish has its own accurate image.
  • Supporting shots shoppers expect: angles, detail, in-scale, in-use and what's-in-the-box. Baymard's 2026 benchmark found 37% of sites still lack an in-scale image and 23% lack a human-model image where one is needed.
  • Seasonal and campaign refreshes for sales events and social posts.
  • Rework after rejections when an image breaks a channel rule.

Creative is a real bottleneck. When Amazon launched AI image generation for advertisers in 2023, it cited its own March 2023 survey: among advertisers unable to build successful campaigns, nearly 75% said building ad creatives and choosing a creative format were their biggest challenges.

Where AI saves time and money

The big platforms have moved fast, and many of their tools are free:

  • Google Product Studio, inside Merchant Center, can create images, change and remove backgrounds, increase resolution and generate product videos at no charge. Google says 80% of surveyed users have been, or expect to be, more efficient using it.
  • Amazon Ads added AI image generation to Posts and Stores in 2024, offering no-cost, scalable access to AI-generated product images and saving the time required for manual editing. Australian availability of these ad tools varies, so check your console.
  • eBay's app lets sellers remove backgrounds and replace them with white, a studio colour or an AI-generated scene.
  • Video: Wyzowl's 2026 survey found 63% of video marketers have used AI to create or edit video, up from 51%.

In practice, the tasks AI handles well are:

  1. Background removal and replacement, turning a reasonable photo into a compliant white-background main image.
  2. Upscaling and clean-up of older or supplier images, within limits.
  3. Channel formatting: generating the right size, crop and framing for each marketplace from one master.
  4. Lifestyle scenes around a real product shot, for secondary images, ads and social.
  5. Seasonal variants without a new shoot.

Where you still need a real shoot

AI can't create accurate information it doesn't have. You still need genuine photography for:

  • The product itself: shape, colour, finish, texture and scale must be true to what ships. The ACCC's guidance says information about products, including images and descriptions of what is offered, must be accurate, whether or not a business intends to mislead.
  • Details and contents: close-ups of materials, ports, labels, and every item in the box.
  • On-model fit for apparel, where fit is the purchase decision. Amazon requires adult clothing main images to show the product on a standing model, and requires metadata disclosure for photorealistic AI-generated people.
  • Used, refurbished or unique items, where the buyer needs to see the exact item.

The most efficient model is a lean, standardised shoot of real products (consistent lighting, a neutral background, a fixed set of angles), followed by AI for everything downstream. Our guide to AI-generated product photos sets out the guardrails.

Building a lean image workflow

  1. Standardise capture. Same lighting, background and angle list for every SKU in a category. Consistency makes AI processing more reliable.
  2. Keep one master record. Store master images with the product data, not in scattered folders and inboxes.
  3. Generate per-channel sets automatically from the masters, using each channel's rules.
  4. Use AI for scenes and refreshes, keeping the product pixels untouched.
  5. Approve once. A person checks accuracy and compliance before anything publishes.
  6. Publish everywhere from one place, so a fix or refresh updates every channel at once.
  7. Track rejections and returns linked to images, and fix the cause in the master.

FAQ

Can AI replace product photography completely?

Not safely. You still need accurate photos of the real product, its details and contents. AI is best used to process, format and extend those photos, not to invent the product.

What's the quickest win for reducing image overhead?

Shoot once to a consistent standard, then automate per-channel resizing, cropping and background compliance from those masters. Most of the repetitive time sits there.

Are supplier images good enough?

Sometimes, as a starting point, if you have the rights to use them and they accurately show the product. They often need cut-outs, upscaling or reformatting to meet each channel's rules, and you may need extra shots for scale and contents.
