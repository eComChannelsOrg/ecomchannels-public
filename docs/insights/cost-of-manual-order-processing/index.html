The Real Cost of Manual Order Processing for Online Retailers

Re-keying orders, stock and tracking costs wages, errors and marketplace penalties. A practical breakdown of manual order processing costs and how to cut it.5 min read7 cited sourcesUpdated 30 October 2026

Key figureMinimum award wages rose 4.75% from 1 July 2026Source: fairwork.gov.au

Manual order processing costs far more than the hours spent typing. Every order that is downloaded, re-keyed, labelled by hand and tracked by copy-and-paste carries wage cost, an error rate of around 1% per keyed value, slower dispatch and exposure to marketplace penalties. With Australian award wages up 4.75% from July 2026, the labour component alone is rising faster than many retailers' margins.

Where the time actually goes

A typical manual workflow for a multichannel seller looks like this:

  1. Log in to each marketplace and the web store, export or read new orders.
  2. Enter or paste orders into a spreadsheet, accounting system or courier portal.
  3. Check stock, then update quantities on every other channel.
  4. Print pick lists and packing slips.
  5. Create labels in a carrier portal, retyping addresses.
  6. Paste tracking numbers back into each marketplace.
  7. Later, match marketplace payouts against orders, fees and refunds.

None of these steps is difficult. The problem is that they repeat for every order and every channel, and each hand-off is a chance to make a mistake. Smartsheet's workplace automation survey found over 40% of workers spent at least a quarter of their week on manual, repetitive tasks, with data collection and data entry among the biggest time sinks. Nearly 60% estimated they could save six or more hours a week if the repetitive parts of their jobs were automated.

Labour costs are rising

Order admin in small retail is usually done by owners, operations staff or casuals on award rates. The Fair Work Commission's 2026 Annual Wage Review increased minimum award wages by 4.75% from 1 July 2026, following a 3.5% increase in July 2025. Two consecutive rises add up to more than 8% in two years on the cost of every hour spent re-keying orders.

A simple way to estimate your own cost: multiply minutes of admin per order by order volume and the loaded hourly rate (wage plus super, leave and on-costs). Then add the cost of errors, which is where manual processing gets expensive.

Errors: small rates, big consequences

Research on data entry gives a useful benchmark. In controlled studies by Barchard and Pace, single data entry produced error rates of around 1%, and visual checking was no more accurate than single entry. In an order context, a 1% error rate on addresses, SKUs or quantities means one problem order in every hundred values keyed, and each order involves many values.

Errors turn into costs quickly:

  • Mis-ships. Descartes' 2025 warehouse benchmark attributes mis-ships mainly to inaccurate data entry and missing validation steps. One business it profiles had been losing an average of $400 per mis-shipped order before tightening its process.
  • Late dispatch. Orders that sit unseen in a portal miss the promised ship date. Amazon's Australian Order Performance policy requires a late shipment rate under 4% and a valid tracking rate above 95%.
  • Cancellations when stock was not updated in time, counted against a 2.5% threshold on Amazon.
  • Customer contacts asking where an order is, which pull staff away from other work.
  • Reconciliation gaps when refunds and fees are not matched to the right orders.

The Descartes report also highlights a quieter cost: processes that live in one or two people's heads. When they are sick or leave, throughput drops and errors rise.

What automation changes

Automation does not need to be ambitious to pay back. The biggest savings usually come from removing re-keying between systems:

  • Orders flow from every channel into one queue automatically.
  • Stock updates push to all channels on each sale.
  • Labels are generated from the order data with no retyping, and tracking returns to the marketplace automatically.
  • Payouts are matched to orders and fees by rules.

Small businesses are already moving. Zapier's 2026 survey of more than 500 SMB owners found 76% use some form of automation, and 94% of those users reported benefits, with improved consistency and fewer errors (44%) and cost savings (44%) the most common. FAQ

How do I calculate my manual order processing cost?

Time a sample of orders end to end, from download to tracking upload. Multiply average minutes per order by monthly volume and your loaded hourly rate, then add the cost of errors: reshipping, refunds, customer contacts and any marketplace fee penalties.

Which step should I automate first?

Usually the one with the most re-keying: getting orders and tracking between marketplaces and the courier. It saves time on every order and removes the errors most likely to affect marketplace metrics.

Does automation mean cutting staff?

Not necessarily. Most small retailers use the time saved to handle growth, add channels or improve customer service without hiring at the same pace as order volume.
