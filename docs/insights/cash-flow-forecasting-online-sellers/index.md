# Cash-Flow Forecasting for Online Sellers: A Practical Guide

URL: https://ecomchannels.com/insights/cash-flow-forecasting-online-sellers/
Category: Finance & reconciliation
Updated: 2026-10-30

> How Australian online sellers can forecast cash flow when marketplaces pay on different cycles: payout timing, peak stock buys, returns and GST.

A cash-flow forecast for an online seller is a week-by-week view of when money will actually arrive from each sales channel and when it will leave for stock, freight, advertising, wages and tax. The key difference from a profit forecast is timing: marketplaces, payment providers and buy now pay later services all pay on their own schedules, so a strong sales month can still leave the bank account short. Building the forecast from reconciled payout data, rather than from sales totals, is what makes it reliable.

## Why profitable online sellers still run short of cash

Cash problems rarely arrive suddenly. The Reserve Bank's 2025 review of company insolvencies found that [inadequate cash flow is the most cited cause of business failure](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/fsr/2025/apr/focus-topic-the-recent-increase-in-company-insolvencies-and-its-implications-for-financial-stability.html), and that insolvency typically follows an extended period of cash-flow difficulty (RBA, 2025). For e-commerce businesses the pressure points are specific:

- **Stock is paid for months before it sells.** Imported stock for peak season is often paid in full well before the first order.
- **Sales income arrives late and net.** Marketplaces hold funds and deduct fees before paying out.
- **Refunds arrive after the sale.** A returned order reverses income weeks after you booked it.
- **Tax is due on a fixed date.** GST collected on sales has to be paid when your BAS is due, whether or not the marketplace has paid you.

Trading conditions add to this. Xero's small business data showed sales growth for Australian [retail trade businesses of just 3.4% year on year in the June 2026 quarter](https://www.xero.com/us/resources/small-business-insights/latest-australia/), among the weaker industries (Xero, 2026). Slower growth leaves less room for timing mistakes.

## Map every channel's payout timing

Start with a simple table: for each channel, how long between the customer paying and the money reaching your bank, and what gets deducted on the way.

- **Amazon.com.au** works on a [14-day payout period, with each settlement closing every 14 days](https://sellercentral.amazon.com.au/help/hub/reference/external/G200801160) (Amazon Seller Central, 2026). Reserves can hold some funds back for a later period.
- **Shopify Payments** in Australia has a [minimum settlement time of 2 business days](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/australia/payouts), but new stores start with longer settlement, and payments captured Friday to Sunday are combined into one payout (Shopify, 2026).
- **Afterpay** pays merchants the order value less its fee, [typically within up to five business days](https://www.afterpay.com/en-AU/business/afterpay-merchant-fees) of an online purchase (Afterpay, 2026).
- **Other marketplaces** each have their own remittance cycles. Take them from your own reconciled history rather than from the seller agreement alone, because actual timing often drifts.

Once you know the lag and the typical deduction rate per channel, you can turn a sales forecast into a cash-receipts forecast.

## Build a 13-week rolling forecast

A 13-week view (one quarter) is long enough to cover a stock order and a BAS cycle, and short enough to stay accurate.

1. **Opening cash.** Your actual bank balance at the start of the week.
2. **Receipts by channel.** Forecast sales per channel, shifted forward by that channel's payout lag and reduced by its effective fee and refund rate.
3. **Payments out.** Supplier invoices by due date, freight and fulfilment, advertising, software, wages and superannuation, and loan repayments.
4. **Tax.** GST and PAYG instalments on their due dates. This is general information; your accountant can confirm your obligations, and the [ATO's Cash Flow Kit](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/starting-registering-or-closing-a-business/running-your-own-business/good-business-habits/cash-flow-kit) frames cash flow around four questions, including whether enough money has been put aside for regular commitments (ATO, 2026).
5. **Closing cash and minimum balance.** Flag any week that drops below the buffer you need.

Each week, replace the forecast with actuals and roll the window forward. The gap between forecast and actual is the most useful number in the model, because it shows which assumptions are wrong.

## Plan for peaks and the returns that follow

Sale events concentrate both spending and cash risk. Australians spent [$2.2 billion online during the 2024 Black Friday sales, up 10% year on year](https://auspost.com.au/content/dam/auspost_corp/media/documents/ecommerce-reports/annual-ecommerce-reports/ecommerce-report-2025.pdf) (Australia Post, 2025). Stock for that period is bought months before, while marketplace payouts for November sales may not land until December.

Returns follow peaks. In the United States, retailers surveyed by the NRF expected their [holiday return rate to be 17% higher than their annual average](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/nrf-and-happy-returns-report-2024-retail-returns-total-890-billion) (NRF, 2024). Australian patterns will differ, but the principle holds: model a higher refund rate for January than for an average month, and keep cash aside for it.

## Make the forecast trustworthy

A forecast is only as good as its inputs. Three habits matter most:

- **Use reconciled payouts, not sales reports.** If you do not know what each channel really pays after fees, refunds and reserves, your receipts line is a guess. Our guide to [marketplace settlement reconciliation](https://ecomchannels.com/insights/marketplace-settlement-reconciliation-explained/) covers how to get that data.
- **Track money owed to you separately.** Short-paid orders, unreleased reserves and pending reimbursements are receivables. Chase them.
- **Review weekly, with one owner.** A forecast nobody updates becomes wrong within a month.

## FAQ

### How far ahead should an online seller forecast cash?

A rolling 13-week forecast suits most retailers, with a separate, rougher 12-month view for peak-season stock buying and financing decisions.

### Should I forecast on sales or on payouts?

Forecast sales first, then convert them into payouts using each channel's real payout lag, fee rate and refund rate. Cash planning needs the payout figure.

### How do I account for marketplace reserves?

Treat reserved funds as money owed to you with an expected release date, and check that they are released in later settlements.
