Influencer and UGC Marketing for Australian Online Retailers

How Australian retailers can use creators and customer content to drive discovery and sales, stay compliant with ACCC rules and measure what actually works.5 min read7 cited sourcesUpdated 30 October 2026

Key figure81% of influencers reviewed by the ACCC made potentially misleading postsSource: accc.gov.au

Influencer and user-generated content (UGC) work because shoppers trust people more than brand ads, and in Australia they are now a core part of how younger customers find products. IAB Australia found 75% of 18 to 39 year olds have discovered brand information from an influencer or creator they follow. The catch is compliance: the ACCC found 81% of influencers it reviewed made posts that raised concerns about potentially misleading advertising. Retailers who pair creator content with clear disclosure and accurate product facts get the benefit without the risk.

Why creator and customer content works

People treat recommendations from other people differently from brand messages. A Stackla survey reported by YouGov found 58% of consumers named UGC as the most authentic form of content, while marketers were more than twice as likely as consumers to say branded content connected most. That gap between what brands think works and what shoppers trust is the opportunity.

More recent social data points the same way. Sprout Social found 55% of social media users are more likely to trust brands committed to publishing content created by humans, and 46% are not comfortable with brands using AI influencers. Real customers and real creators showing real products remain the most credible format.

Spend reflects this. We Are Social reported brands in Australia invested US$520 million in influencer-led campaigns, a 13% increase on the year before. Globally, Meta reported a US$15 billion annual revenue run rate for partnership ads, the format that runs creator content as paid ads.

Influencers, creators and UGC: what's the difference

  • Influencers have an established audience and are usually paid in cash, product or commission to promote a brand.
  • UGC creators make content for a brand to publish on its own channels or in ads. Their audience size matters less than their ability to make convincing, native-looking video.
  • Customer UGC is unpaid content from buyers: reviews, unboxing videos, tagged photos. It is the most trusted, but you need permission before reusing it.

Most small retailers get the best return from a mix: a few micro-creators in their niche, a steady pipeline of UGC-style video for ads, and a simple process for collecting and reusing customer photos and reviews.

Staying on the right side of the ACCC

Consumer law applies fully to social content. The ACCC says the same advertising rules cover social media posts that a business offers incentives to influencers to make, and that information about prices, product images, performance claims, and shipping and delivery times must be accurate.

The ACCC's 2023 sweep reviewed 118 influencers across Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, YouTube, Facebook and Twitch. Concerning posts ranged from 96% of fashion influencers reviewed to 73% in gaming and technology. Common problems were missing disclosure of payments or gifts, vague labels such as "sp" or "spon", and disclosures hidden in the format of the post.

Practical rules for retailers:

  1. Put disclosure in every brief and contract, with clear wording such as "Ad" or "Paid partnership".
  2. Give creators accurate product facts, prices and delivery promises, and check posts before they go live where you can.
  3. Never ask staff, family or paid parties to post reviews without disclosing the connection.
  4. Keep records of what you supplied and what was agreed.

Running a creator program without a big team

  1. Start with fit, not follower count. Look for creators whose audience matches your customers and whose content already looks like your category.
  2. Brief on the problem the product solves. Creators make better content when they know the use case, not just the features.
  3. Get usage rights. Agree upfront whether you can reuse content in ads, on your store and on marketplaces, and for how long.
  4. Feed the best content into your calendar. Strong creator videos can be repurposed as Reels, TikToks, Pins and product page media.
  5. Track with codes and links. Unique discount codes and tagged links let you see which creators drive sales, not just views. See measuring social media ROI.

FAQ

Do micro-influencers work for small Australian retailers?

They often suit small retailers better than large creators because fees are lower and audiences are more specific. A creator with a modest but engaged following in your niche can be a stronger partner than a large general-interest account; test a few and compare sales by code.

Do influencers have to disclose paid posts in Australia?

Yes. The ACCC treats incentivised influencer posts as advertising under the Australian Consumer Law. Payment, free products and other incentives should be clearly disclosed, and vague tags like "sp" are not adequate.

Can I repost customer photos and videos?

Only with permission. Ask the customer, record their agreement, and credit them where appropriate. Never edit customer content in a way that changes its meaning.
