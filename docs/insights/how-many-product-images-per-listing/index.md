# How Many Product Images Per Listing? What Marketplaces Advise

URL: https://ecomchannels.com/insights/how-many-product-images-per-listing/
Category: AI graphics & visual commerce
Updated: 2026-10-30

> Amazon, Google, eBay and TikTok Shop all publish image-count guidance. How many product images to aim for, which shots to include and why order matters.

Aim for six to nine images per listing on most marketplaces: a compliant main image plus five to eight supporting shots, and a video where the channel supports it. That range matches what the platforms themselves recommend (Amazon suggests at least six additional images and one video; Google recommends five or more additional images; TikTok Shop wants at least five for its top listing tier). The number matters less than coverage: each image should answer a different question a shopper would ask in a store.

## What the marketplaces recommend

Platforms publish image-count guidance because complete galleries help their own conversion rates. Current guidance:

| Channel | Recommendation or limit |
|---|---|
| Amazon | One compliant main image plus [at least six additional images and one video](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G1881?locale=en-US) |
| Google Shopping | [At least 5 additional images](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/18353357?hl=en), up to 10 via `additional_image_link` |
| eBay | ["More is more"](https://www.ebay.com.au/sellercentre/create-a-great-listing/photo-tips): up to 24 photos per listing |
| TikTok Shop | [At least five images](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=481891871868714) for a "Good" quality listing |
| Bunnings Marketplace | Primary image plus up to eight additional images, per [ChannelEngine's guide](https://support.channelengine.com/hc/en-us/articles/14858406321437-Bunnings-marketplace-guide) |

Note that on Amazon, [uploading an image doesn't guarantee it will be displayed](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G1881?locale=en-US): Amazon selects and arranges images from multiple sellers on shared listings, favouring compliant, high-quality ones.

## Why more images (usually) help

Shoppers rely on images to replace the in-store inspection, and one photo can't do that. Salsify's 2024 consumer research put it plainly: [having one static image of your product and a few bullet points is not enough](https://www.salsify.com/hubfs/2024/Content/Salsify%20Consumer%20Research%20Report%202024.pdf); shoppers want high-quality images and videos, demonstrations and 360-degree views. In the same survey, 76% said high-quality images were extremely or very important to clicking through from search.

Each extra image is a chance to remove a reason not to buy. Baymard Institute's research shows the most common gaps:

- **Size.** [42% of users try to gauge size from images; 37% of sites lack an in-scale image](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux) (2026).
- **Fit and wear.** [23% of sites don't show worn products on a human model](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux) (2026).
- **Contents.** When a stand mixer listing had no "included accessories" image, [63% of test users missed that it came with three premium accessories](https://baymard.com/research-articles/included-accessories-image) (2019).

## When more images stop helping

More isn't automatically better. Images start to hurt when they:

- **Repeat each other.** Five near-identical angles add scrolling, not information.
- **Hide the important ones.** Baymard found that poor thumbnail truncation can cause [50% to 80% of users to overlook additional images](https://baymard.com/research-articles/included-accessories-image). On your own site, show thumbnails rather than dots: Baymard reports [76% of mobile sites don't use thumbnails for additional images](https://baymard.com/research-articles/always-use-thumbnails-additional-images) (2020), which makes the extra images easy to miss.
- **Slow the page.** On your own storefront, every image is a download. Serve appropriately sized files (see [mobile-first product images](https://ecomchannels.com/insights/mobile-first-product-images/)).
- **Drift from the product.** Images of old versions, other colours or optional extras confuse buyers and invite returns.

## A shot list that covers the questions

Use this as a default and adjust by category:

1. **Main image**: the product alone on a white or light background, following the channel's main-image rules.
2. **Alternate angle**: back, side or three-quarter view.
3. **Detail close-up**: texture, material, controls, stitching, ports.
4. **In-scale or in-use**: the product with a person or in a room.
5. **What's in the box**: all included items laid out and labelled where the channel allows.
6. **Dimensions or key features**: a factual graphic, where text on secondary images is allowed (see [product infographics that sell](https://ecomchannels.com/insights/product-infographics-that-sell/)).
7. **Variant image**: the exact colour or finish being sold. Google asks for [a unique image for each variant](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/18353357?hl=en).
8. **Packaging or certification**: where it informs the decision (gift items, regulated products).
9. **Video**: a short demonstration, where supported.

## Order matters as much as count

Shoppers swipe through galleries in sequence, especially on phones, so the first few images do most of the work. Put the images that answer the biggest purchase questions early: for furniture that's usually scale; for apparel, the on-model shot; for electronics, ports and what's included. Keep the main image as the first image on every channel, since it's the one shown in search results on Amazon and eBay.

## FAQ

### Is there a maximum number of images I should use?

Each channel has its own limit (eBay allows 24, Google accepts 10 additional images). Practically, stop adding images once every common shopper question is answered; duplicates add scrolling without adding information.

### Does image count affect marketplace search ranking?

Marketplaces don't publish ranking formulas, but several link image quality and completeness to visibility. TikTok Shop's top listing tier requires at least five images, and Amazon says high-quality images improve discoverability in search.

### Should every variant have its own images?

Yes. Google asks for a unique image for each variant, showing the exact colour, pattern or finish being sold. Showing the wrong colour is a common cause of returns.
