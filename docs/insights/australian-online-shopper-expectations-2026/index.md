# What Australian Online Shoppers Expect in 2026

URL: https://ecomchannels.com/insights/australian-online-shopper-expectations-2026/
Category: E-commerce trends
Updated: 2026-10-30

> Australian shoppers expect clear delivery dates, delivery choice, reliable tracking, easy returns and accurate stock. The 2026 evidence and how to meet it.

Australian online shoppers in 2026 expect five things: a clear and reliable delivery date, a choice of delivery options, tracking they can rely on, simple returns and stock that is actually available. Price still matters most, but research from Australia Post and Grant Thornton shows that delivery and post-purchase experience often decide whether a shopper buys and whether they come back.

## Delivery: certainty first, speed when it counts

Delivery has the strongest effect on whether people shop online at all. In the [Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026](https://auspost.com.au/content/dam/auspost_corp/media/documents/ecommerce-reports/annual-ecommerce-reports/ecommerce-report-2026.pdf), 73% of shoppers said a good delivery experience makes them more likely to shop online instead of in-store.

What counts as "good" depends on the shopper. Australia Post found 87% of Baby Boomers and 90% of Builders value delivery certainty over speed, while Gen Z is closer to evenly split (49% speed). Speed matters for time-sensitive purchases: 26% expect same-day or next-day delivery for urgent items or special occasions, and 43% would pay extra for it.

Speed also has a measurable effect on conversion. THE ICONIC told Australia Post it sees a 3-10% decrease in conversion for every extra day added to the delivery time shown at checkout. [Grant Thornton's 2025 consumer survey](https://www.grantthornton.com.au/globalassets/1.-member-firms/australian-website/industry/retail/pdfs/gtal_2026_retail_report.pdf) found 40% of Australians think delivery from local retailers should take three days or less. By contrast, 84% expect overseas brands to take at least a week, which gives local sellers a real advantage on speed.

## Choice at checkout

Shoppers want to choose how they receive parcels. Australia Post reports that 69% want a wide range of delivery options at checkout, including collection points, and 32% would choose one retailer over another if it offered out-of-home collection such as Parcel Lockers. The [FY27 Q1 update](https://auspost.com.au/content/dam/auspost/documents/ecommerce/quarterly-updates/oct-2026-quarterly-ecommerce-update.pdf) puts it more simply: 7 in 10 shoppers want collection point options.

Choice only helps if it is shown early. Australia Post found 70% of shoppers are less likely to complete a purchase if delivery communication at checkout is poor, and 71% are less likely to buy if delivery information is unclear.

## Tracking that is accurate and in one place

After purchase, shoppers want certainty more than updates. Australia Post's FY27 Q1 update found 75% prefer regular tracking updates to an "estimated" delivery date. Too many messages also cause problems: 49% say receiving updates from multiple sources is cluttered and confusing, and 3 in 4 want delivery notifications to come from the carrier.

In practice, the tracking number has to reach the customer, and the marketplace, quickly and correctly. A late or missing upload creates support contacts, and on many marketplaces it also counts against your seller performance.

## Returns that are simple, and preferably free

Returns are where many retailers lose repeat business. Grant Thornton found 69% of Australians would not buy from a brand or retailer that doesn't offer free returns, and shoppers who had returned an item rated the experience just 3 out of 10 on average. The most common reason for returning was wrong size or fit (43%), and 26% returned items that were damaged, defective or incorrect.

Getting returns right brings customers back. Australia Post's FY27 Q1 update found 75% of shoppers say a smooth returns process makes them more likely to buy again. Since a share of returns come from wrong or damaged items, accurate picking and better product information help reduce them in the first place.

## Stock that matches what the site says

Availability is easy to overlook. Grant Thornton found 80% of online shoppers turn to a competitor when an item is out of stock, and 34% had cancelled an online order because of a stock shortage. For a seller listing on several channels, inaccurate stock leads to cancelled orders and lost customers, and on marketplaces it can damage your account health.

## Trust and security

Security concerns are growing. PayPal's [2026 eCommerce Index](https://www.paypalobjects.com/marketing/web24/au/PayPal_2026_eCommerce_Index_Infographic.pdf) found 73% of Australians are more concerned about data security than a year ago, and security concerns were the second most common reason for abandoning a purchase (38%). Older shoppers are particularly cautious: Australia Post found 62% of Builders worry about data security online. Familiar payment options, clear policies and a recognisable carrier all help build trust.

These expectations apply to a large and frequent shopper base: [YouGov](https://yougov.com/reports/52243-au-retail-online-delivery-report-2025) found 31% of Australians shop online at least once a week. For the volume picture, see [Australian online shopping statistics 2026](https://ecomchannels.com/insights/australian-online-shopping-statistics-2026/).

## FAQ

### What do Australian online shoppers value most?

Competitive pricing and product quality come first. After that, delivery cost, on-time delivery and product availability have the biggest influence on whether shoppers buy and return, according to Grant Thornton's 2025 survey.

### Do Australian shoppers expect next-day delivery?

Not for every order. Australia Post found 26% expect same-day or next-day delivery for time-sensitive items, and 43% would pay extra for it. Most older shoppers put certainty ahead of speed.

### Are free returns expected in Australia?

For many shoppers, yes. Grant Thornton found 69% would not buy from a retailer without free returns, though 51% said they would pay return postage when free returns are not offered.
