# Building an AI-Ready Retail Team: Skills, Roles and Training

URL: https://ecomchannels.com/insights/building-ai-ready-retail-team/
Category: AI in retail
Updated: 2026-10-30

> How Australian retailers can build an AI-ready team: the skills gap in numbers, which roles change, how to train staff and how to keep people in control of AI.

An AI-ready retail team is not a team of data scientists. It is a team where people know which tasks AI handles, can check its output, and have redesigned their work so AI removes the repetitive parts. Research from Jobs and Skills Australia finds generative AI is more likely to augment jobs than replace them, but the skills gap is real: Microsoft found 71% of Australian business leaders are familiar with AI agents compared with only 31% of employees. Closing that gap through hands-on training on real tasks is the main job.

## The skills gap in numbers

Australian businesses already struggle with skills. The ABS found [35% of businesses reported a skills shortage](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/technology-and-innovation/characteristics-australian-business/2024-25) in 2024-25, with sales and marketing and customer service each cited by 7%. Among businesses with shortages, the most common responses were more on-the-job training (38%) and upskilling existing staff (26%), and 15% took no action at all.

AI adds a new layer. Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index found [71% of Australian business leaders say they are familiar with AI agents but only 31% of employees do](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/features/digital-team-members-will-be-common-in-australian-workplaces-within-five-years-microsoft-report), a wider gap than the global average. NAB research highlighted by the National AI Centre rated SME [workforce readiness for AI at 3.8 out of 10](https://www.ai.gov.au/news-and-insights/blog/building-ai-capability-through-collaboration), calling it the critical barrier, even though one in five SMEs using AI reported transformational improvements.

Leaders are also under pressure. In the same Microsoft research, 47% of Australian leaders said productivity must increase, while 79% of the workforce said they lack the time or energy to do their work.

## Augment, not replace

The main national study on this question, Jobs and Skills Australia's Generative AI Capacity Study, concludes that [Gen AI is more likely to augment jobs than replace them](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/publications/generative-ai-capacity-study-report), with impacts varying across occupations, industries and regions, and with many workers already using AI tools on their own initiative.

Evidence from the workplace supports this. In a study of about 5,000 customer support agents, an AI assistant helped the least experienced agents improve productivity by [35%](https://www.nber.org/digest/20236/measuring-productivity-impact-generative-ai), and agents with two months' tenure performed like those with six months. Attrition among agents with the tool was 8.6% lower. For retailers that rely on casual and seasonal staff, AI that brings new people up to speed faster is valuable.

Employers are still adjusting. McKinsey's 2025 survey found expectations split: [32% of respondents expect AI to reduce their total workforce](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025), 43% expect no change and 13% expect increases. In Australia, Microsoft found 70% of leaders were considering hiring AI-focused roles in the coming year.

## Which retail roles change, and how

| Role | Work AI takes on | Work people keep |
|---|---|---|
| Ecommerce / listings coordinator | Drafting titles, bullets, attributes; resizing images | Approving content, checking claims, category strategy |
| Customer service | Drafting replies, summarising threads, tracking lookups | Complex cases, refunds, tone and judgement |
| Marketing / social | Post drafts, scheduling, variations per platform | Brand voice, campaign ideas, approvals |
| Operations / dispatch | Order routing, label creation, stock sync | Exceptions, carrier relationships, quality |
| Finance | Matching settlements to orders, flagging gaps | Disputes, decisions, reporting |

The pattern is consistent: AI drafts and processes, people approve and handle exceptions. That makes "reviewer" and "exception handler" core skills for every role.

## How to build the team

1. **Name an AI owner.** One person accountable for which tools are used and how; this is also practice one of the government's [guidance for responsible AI](https://ecomchannels.com/insights/ai-risks-governance-australian-retailers/).
2. **Train on real tasks.** Teach staff on your own listings, tickets and orders, not generic courses alone.
3. **Teach review skills.** Staff need to know what AI gets wrong: invented specifications, wrong sizes, unsupported claims.
4. **Redesign one workflow at a time.** McKinsey's high performers stand out for redesigning workflows rather than adding AI on top.
5. **Write simple rules.** What data must never go into public AI tools, what needs approval, and how to report a problem.
6. **Measure and share results.** Hours saved and error rates, reported to the team, build confidence.
7. **Set a human-agent balance.** Microsoft's advice is to keep a human touch where customers expect it and where judgement matters.

## FAQ

### Do I need to hire an AI specialist?

Most small and mid-sized retailers do not. Choose tools built for retail workflows, name an internal owner, and train existing staff to review AI output.

### Will AI replace retail jobs?

Jobs and Skills Australia's study concludes generative AI is more likely to augment jobs than replace them, though tasks within roles will change. Repetitive drafting and data entry shrink; review, exceptions and customer judgement grow.

### What training works best?

Short, hands-on sessions using your own products, tickets and orders, followed by clear rules on approvals and data use. Track time saved so staff can see the benefit.
