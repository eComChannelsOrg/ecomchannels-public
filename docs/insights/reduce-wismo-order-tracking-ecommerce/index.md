# How to Reduce 'Where Is My Order?' Enquiries With Tracking

URL: https://ecomchannels.com/insights/reduce-wismo-order-tracking-ecommerce/
Category: Shipping & delivery
Updated: 2026-10-30

> How Australian online retailers can cut 'where is my order' (WISMO) enquiries with accurate delivery dates, fast tracking upload and clear notifications.

"Where is my order?" (WISMO) enquiries fall when customers can see an accurate delivery date before they buy, receive a tracking number soon after the order ships, and get proactive updates from one clear source. Most WISMO contacts come from uncertainty rather than lost parcels, so the fixes are mostly about faster dispatch, reliable tracking data and fewer, clearer messages.

## Why WISMO matters

Every order-status enquiry costs staff time and usually signals a customer who is already slightly anxious. On marketplaces, the same anxiety turns into buyer messages, negative feedback and claims. Amazon says that [tracked orders have 60 per cent fewer order defects](https://sellercentral.amazon.com.au/help/hub/reference/external/G200847280) and that tracking helps reduce order enquiries from buyers.

You will see confident claims online about what share of support tickets are WISMO. Treat them carefully. Ship24, which sells tracking software, [tried to trace](https://www.ship24.com/blog/why-common-wismo-statistics-are-hard-to-verify) commonly repeated WISMO figures back to primary research and could not find a published study with a sample and methodology behind them. The better approach is to measure your own WISMO rate: order-status contacts divided by orders, tracked by week and by carrier.

## The root causes

Most WISMO enquiries trace back to a handful of causes:

- **Vague or optimistic delivery estimates** shown at checkout.
- **Slow dispatch**, so the order sits unshipped while the customer waits.
- **Late or missing tracking numbers**, especially on marketplaces where tracking must be uploaded.
- **Too many messages from different senders**, which confuse rather than reassure.
- **Carrier exceptions** (failed delivery, address issues, delays) that the customer hears about last.

Australia Post's [eCommerce Report 2026](https://auspost.com.au/content/dam/auspost_corp/media/documents/ecommerce-reports/annual-ecommerce-reports/ecommerce-report-2026.pdf) shows how sensitive shoppers are to this: 70% say poor delivery communication at checkout makes them less likely to buy, and 49% say receiving updates from multiple sources makes the experience feel cluttered and confusing.

## Fix 1: Promise accurately at checkout

Uncertainty starts at checkout. Show an estimated delivery date or range based on your real dispatch cut-off and the carrier's transit time to that postcode. Shippit's [2025 analysis](https://www.shippit.com/delivered-newsletter/fast-delivery-won-peak) found retailer-advertised delivery estimates averaged 5.2 days while actual deliveries averaged 1.7 days. Overly cautious estimates cost conversions, while overly optimistic ones create WISMO. Aim for estimates you hit nearly every time.

## Fix 2: Dispatch fast and upload tracking immediately

The quickest way to stop "where is my order" is to ship it. Same-day dispatch for orders received before a cut-off removes the most common gap. Then make sure tracking reaches the customer straight away. Amazon expects sellers to maintain a [Valid Tracking Rate above 95%](https://sellercentral.amazon.com.au/help/hub/reference/external/GGJVNFDXQT8C3RA8), and other marketplaces have similar requirements. Manual copy-and-paste of tracking numbers is where delays and typos creep in.

## Fix 3: Send fewer, clearer notifications

Customers want updates, but from a trusted source. Australia Post found three in four shoppers want delivery-related notifications to come from the carrier, and its [2025 report](https://auspost.com.au/content/dam/ecommerce-report/australia-post-ecommerce-report-2025.pdf) found that 45% of shoppers actively track parcels, with more than two-thirds going to the delivery provider for that information. Grant Thornton and Power Retail's [2025 survey](https://www.grantthornton.com.au/globalassets/1.-member-firms/australian-website/industry/retail/pdfs/gtal_2025_grant_thornton_power_retail_report.pdf) found half of respondents want real-time updates, with email and SMS still the main channels.

A sensible pattern is: order confirmation with an estimated date; a dispatch message with the tracking link; and exception alerts only when something changes. Australia Post also suggests promoting its tracking app, noting that customers using the app are up to four times less likely to raise delivery-related cases during peak.

## Fix 4: Catch exceptions early and offer self-service

Monitor tracking events for parcels that have not moved, failed delivery attempts and address problems. Contacting the customer first, with a new date or a collection option, turns a complaint into a reassurance. Offering Parcel Lockers or other collection points also removes many failed deliveries; see our guide to [parcel lockers and click-and-collect](https://ecomchannels.com/insights/parcel-lockers-click-and-collect-australia/).

Then give customers a self-service status page. A simple order-status page on your webstore, linked from every email, answers most questions without a support ticket. It should show the order status, tracking link, carrier and expected delivery date in plain language.

## FAQ

### What does WISMO stand for?

WISMO stands for "where is my order". It covers any customer contact asking about the status or expected arrival of an order that has already been placed.

### What is a good WISMO rate?

There is no reliable published benchmark. Measure order-status contacts as a share of orders, track it weekly, and compare by carrier, region and channel to find where the problems are.

### Do marketplaces penalise missing tracking?

Yes. Amazon, for example, expects a Valid Tracking Rate above 95% and may restrict selling in a category if it falls below that, and eBay uses tracking to assess whether shipments were late.
