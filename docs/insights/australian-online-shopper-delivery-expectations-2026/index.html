What Australian Shoppers Expect From Delivery in 2026

Australian shoppers want reliable, low-cost delivery, clear dates and more choice at checkout. Here is what the 2025-26 research says and how to respond.5 min read5 cited sourcesUpdated 30 October 2026

Key figure73% of shoppers say a good delivery experience makes them more likely to shop onlineSource: auspost.com.au

Australian online shoppers expect delivery that is affordable, dependable and clearly explained before they pay. Speed matters most to younger shoppers and for urgent purchases, but across the market cost and certainty still outrank raw speed. Retailers that show accurate delivery dates, offer a choice of delivery and collection options, and then hit those promises consistently are the ones that win repeat orders.

Delivery now shapes whether people buy online at all

Delivery is no longer the last step of the purchase. It is part of the reason people choose to shop online in the first place. Australia Post's eCommerce Report 2026 found that 73% of shoppers say a good delivery experience makes them more likely to shop online instead of in-store. The same report puts 2025 online spending at $82.6 billion, up 14% on the previous year, across 9.8 million households.

The flip side is that delivery problems damage loyalty. In a 2025 survey of 1,018 Australian consumers, Grant Thornton and Power Retail found that unreasonable delivery costs would stop 40% of shoppers buying from a retailer again, and a late delivery would stop 23%.

Cost and certainty usually beat speed

There is a gap between what many retailers assume and what most shoppers say. ShipStation research reported by Power Retail in 2025 found that only 11% of Australian shoppers expect next-day delivery, compared with 19% globally, while 40% prioritise low delivery costs over speed and 63% would abandon a purchase if shipping fees were too high.

Australia Post's 2026 data adds a generational lens. When asked to choose between speed and certainty, 49% of Gen Z and 46% of Millennials leaned towards speed, but 70% of Gen X, 87% of Baby Boomers and 90% of Builders chose certainty. Most shoppers will wait a few days if they know when the parcel will arrive and it turns up as promised.

On timing, the Grant Thornton survey found 39% of shoppers expect Australian retailers to deliver in three to five days, 15% will wait up to a week, and 40% think delivery should take three days or less. Australia Post's 2025 report similarly found most shoppers expect delivery within two to five days.

Choice at checkout is now expected

Shoppers want to decide how and where they receive parcels. According to the 2026 report, 69% like getting a wide range of delivery options at checkout, including out-of-home collection and returns, and 32% would switch retailers to get access to collection points such as Parcel Lockers. For a deeper look at those options, see our guide to parcel lockers and click-and-collect.

Choice also covers price. A practical structure many retailers use is a free or low-cost standard option, a paid express option, and a collection option. That lets cost-sensitive buyers pay less and time-sensitive buyers pay more, without forcing a single trade-off on everyone.

Clear communication matters before and after purchase

Delivery communication starts on the product page, not after dispatch. Australia Post found that 70% of shoppers say poor delivery communication at checkout makes them less likely to complete a purchase. After the order, 49% say updates from multiple sources feel cluttered and confusing, and three in four want delivery notifications to come from the carrier.

The practical lesson is to show an honest estimated delivery date early, keep it consistent through checkout and confirmation, and pass accurate tracking to the customer quickly. Our article on reducing "where is my order" enquiries covers this in more detail.

Sales periods raise the bar

Expectations tighten during sale events. Australia Post's quarterly update reports that one in two shoppers expect faster delivery during sales periods and half would switch retailers to get it. Peak season is also when dispatch backlogs, late shipments and missed carrier cut-offs are most likely, so it pays to plan carrier capacity and dispatch staffing before volumes spike.

Key figures

  • 73% of shoppers say a good delivery experience makes them more likely to shop online rather than in-store (Australia Post, 2026).
  • 70% say poor delivery communication at checkout makes them less likely to complete a purchase (Australia Post, 2026).
  • 40% of Australian shoppers prioritise low delivery costs over speed, and 63% would abandon a purchase if shipping fees were too high (Power Retail, 2025).
  • Unreasonable delivery costs would stop 40% of consumers buying from a retailer again (Grant Thornton and Power Retail, 2025).
  • 56% of shoppers ranked free shipping as their top delivery preference in 2024 (Australia Post, 2025).

What retailers should do now

  1. Publish delivery promises you can keep. Base estimated dates on your real dispatch times and carrier transit times, not on hopeful averages.
  2. Offer at least three choices. Standard, express and a collection option cover most shopper needs.
  3. Make costs visible early. Show delivery fees or free-shipping thresholds on product and cart pages, not only at the final step.
  4. Dispatch the same day where possible. Fast dispatch shortens delivery times without paying for premium services.
  5. Measure what customers experience. Track on-time delivery, damaged parcels and delivery-related contacts by carrier and by region.

FAQ

How fast do Australian shoppers expect online orders to arrive?

Most expect two to five days for standard delivery. A 2025 Grant Thornton survey found 39% expect three to five days and 40% expect three days or less, while only 11% of Australian shoppers expect next-day delivery according to ShipStation research.

Is free delivery more important than fast delivery?

For most Australian shoppers, yes. Australia Post found free shipping was the top delivery preference for 56% of shoppers, and older shoppers in particular value delivery certainty over speed. Younger shoppers are more willing to pay for speed when it matters.

What is the biggest delivery mistake online retailers make?

Unclear or unreliable delivery information. Hidden costs and vague timeframes at checkout drive abandonment, and late or damaged deliveries drive customers to switch retailers.
