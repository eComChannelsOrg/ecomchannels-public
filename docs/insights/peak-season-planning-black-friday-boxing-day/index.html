Peak Season Planning: Black Friday to Boxing Day in Australia

Black Friday is now Australia's biggest sales event. 2025 peak data, when shoppers start buying, and a practical plan for stock, listings and dispatch.5 min read8 cited sourcesUpdated 30 October 2026

Key figureAustralia Post delivered more than 110 million parcels in November-December 2025, up 7.6%Source: auspost.com.au

Australia's peak trading season now runs from late October to the end of December, and Black Friday has overtaken Boxing Day as its main event. The Australian Retailers Association and Roy Morgan forecast a record $6.8 billion in spending over the 2025 Black Friday to Cyber Monday weekend, against $1.6 billion forecast for Boxing Day. Australia Post delivered more than 110 million parcels in November and December 2025. Preparing for peak means stock, listings and dispatch need to be ready before November.

How big is peak in Australia?

Black Friday to Cyber Monday. The ARA and Roy Morgan forecast $6.8 billion in spending over the four days from 28 November to 1 December 2025, up 4% on 2024. They expected about 6 million Australians to take part, with average planned spending of $804 per person. The same release cites Deloitte's expectation that 44% of Australian retailers would participate.

Online during the cyber weekend. Australia Post's January 2026 update reports online purchases over the 2025 cyber weekend were up 6.3% year on year. Across November and December, it delivered more than 110 million parcels (up 7.6%), about 3,075 parcels every minute. A year earlier, Australia Post's 2025 report recorded $2.2 billion spent online during the 2024 Black Friday sales, up 10%.

Boxing Day. The ARA and Roy Morgan forecast $1.6 billion in Boxing Day spending in 2025 (up 4.3%) and $3.832 billion over the week from 25 to 31 December. Household goods were expected to lead at $476 million.

The Age reported on Boxing Day 2025 that the day's position as the biggest shopping day had passed to Black Friday, and that discounting now stretches across November.

Shoppers plan ahead and wait for the deal

Peak demand is concentrated because shoppers deliberately wait for it. In the Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026, 73% of consumers said they wait for sales events before purchasing, and 96% of Gen Z hold out for events such as Black Friday and Cyber Monday.

Timing is spread out, though. Australia Post's FY27 Q1 update found 9 in 10 consumers planned to shop the 2026 sales despite cost-of-living pressure. Most begin Christmas shopping in early November, but around one in three start earlier. Younger shoppers start first, and Builders tend to wait until December. Visa's data on the 2025 season found retail spending rose 5% in the seven weeks from 1 November and online sales grew 15%, with signs that shoppers bought Christmas gifts earlier.

For planning, this means demand rises from late October (including mid-November events such as Click Frenzy), peaks over the Black Friday weekend, and continues through Christmas cut-off dates and Boxing Day clearance.

Price wins attention; delivery wins the sale

Discounts bring shoppers in, but Australia Post's research shows delivery often decides who gets the order:

  • 46% of shoppers would switch retailers for better value during peak sales.
  • One in two expect faster delivery during sales periods, and half would switch retailers to get it.
  • 71% are less likely to buy if delivery information at checkout is unclear.

Deep discounts have costs as well. The Age reported concerns from the Council of Small Business Organisations Australia that the expectation of heavy discounting is costly for smaller retailers, some of whom say they sell at a loss to take part. Discount selectively, and check your margin per channel after fees.

A practical peak checklist

  1. Lock stock and sync it everywhere. Overselling during peak causes cancellations, unhappy customers and marketplace penalties. Use one stock figure across every channel.
  2. Update listings and prices in bulk before the event. Prepare promotional pricing, titles and images ahead of time so changes go live on schedule on every marketplace.
  3. Stress-test fulfilment. Australia Post advises finding bottlenecks in stock, pick and pack, and dispatch before peak. Consider extending pick-and-pack hours, since many shoppers order in the evening.
  4. Publish cut-off dates and delivery options early. Show carrier cut-offs and collection options on product pages and at checkout.
  5. Automate tracking and claims. Peak volume multiplies "where is my order" contacts, and fast tracking uploads reduce them.
  6. Plan for January returns. Australia Post notes that clear returns processes pay off again in January, when returns arrive.

Value-focused shopping behaviour also continues outside peak; see cost of living and value shopping.

FAQ

When does peak season start in Australia?

Australia Post's research shows most shoppers start Christmas shopping in early November and around one in three start earlier. In practice, demand rises from late October, peaks over the Black Friday to Cyber Monday weekend and continues through Christmas cut-offs to Boxing Day.

Is Black Friday bigger than Boxing Day in Australia?

Yes, on current forecasts. The ARA and Roy Morgan forecast $6.8 billion over the four-day 2025 Black Friday to Cyber Monday weekend, compared with $1.6 billion on Boxing Day and $3.8 billion across the whole post-Christmas week.

What is the biggest operational risk during peak?

Overselling and late dispatch. Both lead to cancellations and unhappy customers, and on marketplaces both count against your seller performance. Synced stock and automated label and tracking workflows reduce both risks.
