A product title that converts tells the shopper and the search engine exactly what the product is in the first few words: product type, brand and the one or two details buyers filter on, such as size, quantity, material or compatibility. Each marketplace has its own length limit and formatting rules, so the same product needs a slightly different title on Amazon, eBay, TikTok Shop, Bunnings and Google. Write for the reader first, keep the most important details at the front, and stay inside each channel's rules.

## Why titles carry so much weight

The title is usually the strongest relevance signal in marketplace search and the first thing a shopper reads on the results page. It affects both whether your listing appears and whether it gets clicked. eBay Australia tells sellers that titles of [60 to 80 characters are 2.1 times more likely to sell](https://www.ebay.com.au/sellercentre/private-selling/listing-an-item) and recommends including brand, colour, size, material and special details (eBay Australia).

Shoppers also scan. Google notes that users will usually notice only [the first 70 or fewer characters of a title](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324415?hl=en), depending on screen size, and advises putting the most important details first (Google Merchant Center Help). On small screens, titles are often cut even shorter.

## Title rules by channel

| Channel | Length guidance | Notable rules |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon | Up to 200 characters for most categories | Certain special characters banned; no word more than twice (prepositions, articles and conjunctions excepted) |
| eBay | 80 characters | Use as many as you can; include brand, model, colour, style, model number |
| TikTok Shop (US guidance) | 40 to 150 characters | Avoid repeated words, variants in the title and subjective marketing terms |
| Google Shopping | Up to 150 characters | Important details first; AI-generated titles go in a separate structured title field |
| Bunnings Marketplace (via ChannelEngine guide) | Up to 128 characters | Suggested format: Brand, Size, Colour, Title, Product type |

## A simple title formula

Most strong titles follow the same order, adjusted per channel:

**Brand + product type + key differentiator + size/quantity + material or colour + compatibility or model**

Examples of the logic (not real products):

- Amazon: "Brand Stainless Steel Insulated Water Bottle 1L, Leak-Proof Lid, Keeps Drinks Cold 24 Hours, Black"
- eBay (80 characters): "Brand Insulated Water Bottle 1L Stainless Steel Leak Proof Black"
- Bunnings style: "Brand 1L Black Stainless Steel Insulated Water Bottle"

Notice what is missing: words like "best", "sale", "free shipping" or "hot". Many marketplaces discourage promotional language in titles, and TikTok Shop specifically warns against subjective marketing terms (TikTok Shop, 2026).

## Common title mistakes

- **Leading with the brand when nobody searches it.** If your brand is unknown, the product type should appear very early so the title is understood at a glance.
- **Keyword stuffing.** Amazon now limits repeated words, and its [seller guidance](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.za/welcome/learn/seller-blog/how-to-improve-amazon-listings) warns that excessive repetition harms readability and can depress click-through and conversion.
- **Putting variants in the title.** Sizes and colours often belong in variation attributes, not in a parent title.
- **One title everywhere.** An Amazon-length title will be truncated or rejected on eBay and Bunnings.
- **Missing the buyer's words.** Use the terms customers actually type. Our guide to [keyword research for marketplaces](/insights/keyword-research-for-marketplaces/) shows how to find them.

## How to test and improve titles

Change one element at a time and compare impressions, click-through rate and conversion over a comparable period. TikTok Shop reports that sellers adopting its recommended title keywords can see search exposure increase [within 7 days](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=1664446336747310&lang=en) (TikTok Shop, 2026). Amazon Brand Analytics and eBay's listing tools offer similar search data. See [measuring listing performance](/insights/measuring-listing-performance-ctr-conversion/) for the metrics to watch.

## FAQ

### Should I use the full character limit?

Use the space if it adds useful, searchable detail, but keep the first 60 to 70 characters self-explanatory because that is what most shoppers see. Never pad a title with repeated or irrelevant words.

### Should the brand come first?

The Bunnings format suggested in ChannelEngine's guide puts the brand first, and eBay recommends including it. If your brand is not well known, make sure the product type follows immediately.

### Can I use the same title on my own website?

You can start from the same core, but your storefront has more room for brand voice. Google Shopping has its own rules, so treat it as a separate channel.
