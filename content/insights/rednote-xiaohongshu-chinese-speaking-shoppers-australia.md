RedNote, known in Chinese as Xiaohongshu or "Little Red Book", is a lifestyle and product-discovery app where people search for honest reviews and guides before they spend. It has around [300 million monthly active users](https://www.straitstimes.com/business/companies-markets/tiktok-rival-xiaohongshu-expects-profit-to-triple-to-3-9-billion), and it is widely used by Chinese-speaking communities outside mainland China, including in Australia, where [732,000 residents were born in China](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/australias-population-country-birth/latest-release). For Australian retailers, RedNote is a way to answer the questions Chinese-speaking shoppers, students and visitors are already searching for.

## What RedNote is and how people use it

RedNote looks like a mix of Instagram and Pinterest: a grid of photo and short-video "notes" with likes, comments and saves. The difference is intent. As one Australian agency put it in Inside Retail, [people use it like a search engine](https://insideretail.com.au/business/why-rednote-belongs-in-your-australia-and-new-zealand-marketing-plan-202610): they post honest reviews, practical guides and itineraries, and others search, save and question them before buying.

That makes RedNote a research platform. Notes that compare products, explain sizing, show real-world use or list "where to buy in Sydney" tend to be saved and revisited. Polished brand ads count for less than content that looks genuine and helpful. Comments and direct messages are active, so brands should expect questions and answer them.

## Who you reach in Australia

The audience is real and growing. The ABS reports that at 30 June 2025, people born in China were [Australia's third-largest overseas-born group at 732,000, up 32,000 on the year before](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/australias-population-country-birth/latest-release). In the 2021 Census, [Mandarin was the most common language other than English used at home, by 685,274 people (2.7% of the population), with Cantonese used by 295,281 (1.2%)](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/cultural-diversity-australia).

Visitors and students add to that. Inside Retail, quoting Tourism Research Australia, reports that [China was Australia's second-largest inbound market in 2025, with visitation up 17% and spend up 39% to A$11.2 billion](https://insideretail.com.au/business/why-rednote-belongs-in-your-australia-and-new-zealand-marketing-plan-202610), and that 24% of Chinese visitors come for education. Students living here for years buy groceries, homewares, electronics, beauty and fashion like any other resident.

RedNote also reaches [Chinese-speaking communities in Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore and Malaysia](https://insideretail.com.au/business/why-rednote-belongs-in-your-australia-and-new-zealand-marketing-plan-202610), which matters for retailers who ship internationally.

For most retailers the point is not a separate market but a separate research path. A Chinese-speaking shopper in Sydney may compare your product on RedNote, then buy it on a marketplace or your website. If nothing about your brand appears in that search, a competitor's notes answer the question instead. A small number of helpful, accurate notes can be enough to appear where none of your rivals do.

## Categories that suit RedNote

RedNote content clusters around lifestyle decisions. Categories that tend to fit include:

- beauty, skincare and health products, including Australian-made goods
- fashion, footwear and accessories
- homewares and small appliances for people setting up a new home
- baby and parenting products
- food, gifts and local specialities
- travel retail and experiences

The common thread is products where buyers want real-world proof and practical answers before spending.

## How to get started on RedNote

1. **Research before posting.** Search RedNote for your category in Simplified Chinese and English. Note the questions people ask, the terms they use and what content gets saved.
2. **Write for questions, not campaigns.** Notes such as "how to choose the right size", "what's actually in this product" or "where to buy in Melbourne" fit the platform better than announcements.
3. **Use native-language content.** Most RedNote content is in Chinese. Have captions and text overlays written or reviewed by a fluent speaker, not just machine-translated.
4. **Show real use.** RedNote users [trust content that looks real over content that looks expensive](https://insideretail.com.au/business/why-rednote-belongs-in-your-australia-and-new-zealand-marketing-plan-202610), so favour images and short videos of the product in Australian homes and settings.
5. **Work with local creators.** Inside Retail notes that [creator tiers in Australia look different: a "mega" creator here might have 50,000 to 200,000 followers](https://insideretail.com.au/business/why-rednote-belongs-in-your-australia-and-new-zealand-marketing-plan-202610), which is mid-tier in China. Smaller local creators can be cost-effective.
6. **Answer comments and messages.** Treat engagement as customer service. Questions in comments are often the strongest buying signals.
7. **Link the journey.** Make sure shoppers can find your products afterwards, whether on your store or a marketplace, with clear delivery information.

Australian Consumer Law applies regardless of language: [prices, product claims and delivery information on social media must be accurate](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/social-media-promotions), and paid creator posts should be disclosed. ## FAQ

### Is RedNote the same as Xiaohongshu?

Yes. Xiaohongshu ("Little Red Book") is the Chinese name; RedNote is the English name used outside China. It is a lifestyle, review and product-discovery app.

### Can Australian businesses use RedNote?

Yes. Australian businesses can open accounts, publish notes and work with creators. Content is mostly in Chinese, so native-language writing and review are important.

### Do I need to sell on RedNote to benefit?

No. Many Australian retailers use RedNote for discovery and trust, then send shoppers to their own store, a marketplace or a physical location to buy.
