Yes, the best available evidence says product video and interactive 360 or 3D views lift sales. Amazon reports that listings saw an average 24% increase in sales after adding their first shoppable video, and peer-reviewed research has found 360-degree rotatable images raise purchase intention compared with static photos. The gains come from answering questions static images can't (how a product moves, works, fits or folds), so short, practical videos beat polished brand films on marketplaces.

## What the data says about product video

Amazon has published the most direct marketplace data. Its seller guidance says detail pages that added their first shoppable video saw an [average 24% increase in sales compared with those without videos](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-product-video) (2025), with category averages including 32.7% for toys, 30.5% for apparel and 25.8% for beauty. Its Seller Central video guide cites a [23.8% average sales increase versus listings without video](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GWJRQF3C34G4PRA2), based on internal data from January to June 2024. Amazon now recommends at least six additional images [and one video](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G1881?locale=en-US) on every listing.

These are Amazon's own figures, measured on Amazon, so treat them as directional for other channels. But consumer research points the same way. Wyzowl's 2026 survey found [85% of people have been convinced to buy a product or service by watching a video, and 63% would most like to learn about a product by watching a short video](https://www.wyzowl.com/video-marketing-statistics/). In Salsify's 2024 research, shoppers describing engaging buying experiences repeatedly asked for [high-quality images and videos, product demonstrations and 360-degree views](https://www.salsify.com/hubfs/2024/Content/Salsify%20Consumer%20Research%20Report%202024.pdf).

## What makes a product video work

Amazon's analysis of top-performing videos found each answered common customer questions [in under 35 seconds](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-product-video) through visual demonstration rather than lengthy explanation. Its category examples are a useful template:

- **Toys and games:** show assembly and the finished result.
- **Apparel and wearables:** show fit, comfort and sizing context, which Amazon links to fewer returns.
- **Home:** show the product in a realistic room so shoppers understand scale and placement.
- **Beauty and consumables:** explain how to use it and what to expect.

Practical rules that hold across channels:

1. **Lead with the product in use** in the first few seconds, so the point lands even if a viewer stops early.
2. **Design for sound off.** Use on-screen captions for key facts where the channel allows.
3. **Shoot vertical and square cuts** as well as landscape, since social and mobile placements favour them.
4. **Stay factual.** Video is advertising under Australian Consumer Law. Don't show uses the product isn't rated for.
5. **Follow channel rules.** Amazon rejects videos with external links, URLs or promotional calls to action.

## 360-degree spins and 3D models

A 360 spin lets shoppers rotate the product; a 3D model goes further, allowing zoom from any angle and, with augmented reality (AR), placing the product in their own room.

The academic evidence is encouraging. In three experiments published in the *Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services*, Kim, Baek and Yoon found participants reported [higher purchase intention for a 360-degree rotatable image than for a two-dimensional static image](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/joreco/v55y2020ics0969698919310902.html) (2020). The effect ran through "sensory vividness", and it weakened when shoppers were distracted or busy, which is a reminder that 360 content supports, rather than replaces, a clear main image.

Platform data is stronger still, though it comes from vendors with an interest. Shopify says that, according to its merchant data, [products with AR and 3D content see conversion rates up to 94% higher](https://www.shopify.com/au/blog/digital-transformation-trends-in-retail) than comparable products without them. Shopify also reports that pet kennel maker Gunner Kennels saw a 40% increase in order conversion and a 5% reduction in returns after adding 3D and AR.

360 and 3D are worth the most for products where shape, size and finish drive the decision: furniture, homewares, footwear, bags, tools, electronics and large items where returns are expensive.

## Where video and 360 fit on Australian channels

Support varies. Amazon accepts shoppable video on detail pages. Social commerce channels such as TikTok Shop are video-first. Many retailer-run marketplaces focus on still images, so check each seller portal before investing in formats a channel won't display. Your own storefront is where you have full control: 360 viewers and 3D models can be embedded directly.

Australia Post's 2026 eCommerce report quotes TOMORROW founder Jordan Berke on the direction of travel: ["Content-driven experiences are increasingly going to take share of customer spend"](https://auspost.com.au/content/dam/auspost_corp/media/documents/ecommerce-reports/annual-ecommerce-reports/ecommerce-report-2026.pdf), whether through live selling or short-form video.

## FAQ

### How long should a product video be?

Short. Amazon found its top-performing product videos answered key customer questions in under 35 seconds. Show the product working in the first few seconds and design it to make sense with the sound off.

### Is 360 or 3D worth it for small retailers?

Start with the products where shoppers most need to see shape, size and finish, and where returns are costly, such as furniture, footwear and homewares. Test on your own storefront first, where you control the viewer.

### Do I need professional video production?

Not necessarily. Clear, well-lit demonstrations shot on a modern phone can work, provided they are accurate and follow each channel's rules. Factual and helpful matters more than cinematic.
